# HR Analytics Domain Study

## 1. Introduction

HR Analytics is a discipline that applies data analysis, statistical methods, and evidence-based reasoning to Human Resources. Its goal is to help organizations make informed decisions about people: hiring, development, performance, retention, and overall workforce strategy. By transforming HR data into actionable insights, HR Analytics strengthens the connection between business objectives and people management, enabling companies to improve efficiency, support employee well‑being, and build sustainable organizational practices.

## 2. What Is HR Analytics?

HR Analytics is the systematic analysis of HR-related data aimed at:

* improving hiring processes;
* increasing employee performance and productivity;
* reducing turnover;
* optimizing workforce costs;
* creating a fairer, more supportive work environment.

In short, HR Analytics helps answer the question:
**How can we use data to make work better for both employees and organizations?**

## 3. Key Focus Areas

### **Talent Acquisition Analytics**

How can we improve hiring? Which channels bring the best candidates? How much does it cost to fill a role? Analytics helps make recruitment faster, more efficient, and more cost-effective.

### **Employee Performance & Productivity**

How do we measure employee effectiveness? What factors influence performance? Analytics helps identify productivity patterns, strengths within teams, and areas where support is needed.

### **Retention & Turnover Analysis**

Why do employees leave? Who is at risk? Data-driven models help forecast turnover and enable proactive retention strategies.

### **Employee Engagement**

Engagement analytics includes survey results, sentiment, and behavioral indicators. It helps companies understand motivation, satisfaction, and the drivers of a healthy workplace.

### **Compensation & Benefits Analytics**

Are compensation packages fair and competitive? Analytics helps optimize spending and ensure pay equity across the organization.

### **Workforce Planning**

What talent will be needed in 6–12 months? Which skills should be prioritized? Workforce planning uses forecasting to prevent hiring gaps and support long-term strategy.

## 4. Typical Data Used in HR Analytics

"Having worked in HR, I know how much valuable data exists across different systems. Below are common data sources used in HR Analytics:" — **Yevheniia Rudenko**

* **ATS (Applicant Tracking System):** applications, hiring stages, candidate sources.
* **HRIS:** employee records, contracts, start dates, job titles.
* **Performance data:** KPI, OKRs, evaluation results.
* **Surveys:** engagement, satisfaction, eNPS.
* **Payroll:** compensation, bonuses, benefits.
* **Learning & Development:** participation in trainings, results, skill assessments.

In HR Analytics, the key is not only collecting data — but interpreting it correctly.

## 5. Tools and Technologies

An HR analyst typically works with tools that cover the full data lifecycle:

* **Excel** — foundational analysis and dashboards.
* **SQL** — querying databases.
* **Python** — statistical analysis, modeling, automation.
* **Tableau / Power BI** — dashboards and visual insights.
* **HRIS platforms** such as Workday, BambooHR, or SAP SuccessFactors.

The combination of HR experience and analytical skills creates a powerful toolkit.

## 6. Use Cases

A few common business problems solved with HR Analytics:

* **Identifying reasons for turnover** using predictive models.
* **Optimizing the hiring funnel** to improve conversion rates and reduce costs.
* **Forecasting hiring needs** based on business growth.
* **Compensation benchmarking** to detect anomalies or inequalities.
* **Employee segmentation** to understand different groups' needs and behaviors.

These use cases transform HR from an administrative function into a strategic partner.

## 7. Ethical Considerations

"My HR experience taught me how sensitive employee data is and why ethical considerations must come first." — **Yevheniia Rudenko**
Ethical analytics requires:

* strict confidentiality and data protection;
* avoiding bias in models and reports;
* using data to support employees, not to control them;
* transparency about how data is used.

Ethics is the foundation of responsible HR Analytics.

## 8. Conclusion

HR Analytics plays a crucial role in modern organizations by transforming HR data into meaningful insights that support strategic decision-making. Through the analysis of workforce trends, performance indicators, and employee experience metrics, HR Analytics enables organizations to build stronger, more efficient, and people-centered environments. As the field continues to evolve, the integration of data-driven practices within HR ensures a more proactive, fair, and sustainable approach to managing talent.
