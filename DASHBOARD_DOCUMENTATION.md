### 🎯 Purpose
The Overview Dashboard provides real-time insights into workforce metrics, helping HR teams monitor employee satisfaction, retention, and organizational health at a glance.

#### 1. Total Employees (1,470)

**How to Use:**
- Monitor quarterly to identify growth patterns
- Compare with budget headcount projections
- Use for capacity planning and infrastructure decisions

#### 3. Attrition Rate (16.1%)
**What it measures:** Percentage of employees who left the company  
**Formula:** (Employees Left / Total Employees) × 100  
**Trend:** ⚠️ Above industry avg (industry: 12-15%)  
**Interpretation:**
- Higher than optimal retention
- Indicates potential cultural or compensation issues
- Significant cost impact (replacement cost: 50-200% of salary)

**Actionable Insights:**
- 🚨 **Priority Action Needed** - Above industry benchmark
- 💰 **Cost Impact:** Each lost employee costs $15k-$100k to replace
- 🎯 **Target:** Reduce to 12% within 6 months

**How to Use:**
1. **Immediate Actions:**
   - Conduct exit interviews
   - Analyze attrition by department
   - Review compensation competitiveness

2. **Strategic Initiatives:**
   - Implement retention programs
   - Improve onboarding experience
   - Enhance career development opportunities

3. **Monitoring:**
   - Track monthly attrition rate
   - Compare voluntary vs involuntary attrition
   - Monitor early tenure departures (0-12 months)

**Attrition Risk Levels:**
```
0-10%:   Excellent (Healthy turnover)
10-15%:  Good (Industry standard)
15-20%:  Concerning (Action needed) ← Current position
20-30%:  High Risk (Critical intervention)
30%+:    Crisis (Emergency measures)
```

## Employee Questionary

### 🎯 Purpose
Bi-annual survey to capture employee sentiment, identify issues early, and measure the effectiveness of HR initiatives.

### 📝 Survey Design

**Frequency:** Every 6 months (January & July)  
**Completion Time:** 5-10 minutes  
**Response Rate Target:** >85%  
**Anonymity:** Optional (anonymous by default, can opt-in to share ID)

### 📊 Question Categories

#### 1. Environment Satisfaction (1-4 scale)

**Question:** "How satisfied are you with your work environment?"

**What We're Measuring:**
- Physical workspace quality
- Equipment and technology
- Office atmosphere and culture
- Flexibility (remote/hybrid options)
- Safety and well-being

**Response Scale:**
```
1 = Low       - Major issues, needs immediate attention
2 = Medium    - Some concerns, room for improvement
3 = High      - Generally satisfied, minor tweaks
4 = Very High - Excellent, best-in-class
```

**Why It Matters:**
- Immediate impact on daily experience
- Affects productivity and morale
- Often quick wins available
- Competitive differentiator in recruitment

**Red Flags (score <2):**
- Poor lighting/temperature control
- Outdated equipment
- Noise disruptions
- Inadequate remote work setup
- Safety concerns

**Action Threshold:**
- Department avg <2.5: Immediate intervention
- Individual score of 1: Personal follow-up within 48 hours

---

#### 2. Job Satisfaction (1-4 scale)

**Question:** "How satisfied are you with your current role and responsibilities?"

**What We're Measuring:**
- Role clarity and expectations
- Work-life balance
- Challenge and growth
- Meaningfulness of work
- Autonomy and empowerment

**Response Scale:**
```
1 = Low       - Strongly considering leaving
2 = Medium    - Staying but unfulfilled
3 = High      - Engaged and productive
4 = Very High - Thriving, advocating for company
```

**Why It Matters:**
- #1 predictor of retention
- Drives discretionary effort
- Impacts customer satisfaction (for client-facing roles)
- Influences referral quality

**Correlation Insights:**
```
Job Satisfaction → Attrition Risk
4.0: <5% attrition
3.0-3.9: 10-15% attrition
2.0-2.9: 20-30% attrition
<2.0: >40% attrition (urgent intervention)
```

**Action Threshold:**
- Score <2: Immediate 1-on-1 with manager + HR
- Score of 1: Retention conversation, address concerns

---

#### 3. Relationship Satisfaction (1-4 scale)

**Question:** "How satisfied are you with workplace relationships and team collaboration?"

**What We're Measuring:**
- Manager relationship quality
- Peer collaboration
- Cross-functional teamwork
- Psychological safety
- Communication effectiveness

**Response Scale:**
```
1 = Low       - Isolation, conflict, poor leadership
2 = Medium    - Functional but could be warmer
3 = High      - Collaborative, supportive
4 = Very High - High-trust, high-performance team
```

**Why It Matters:**
- "People don't quit jobs, they quit managers"
- Team cohesion drives innovation
- Psychological safety enables risk-taking
- Culture is lived through relationships

**Manager Scorecard:**
```
If manager's team avg:
>3.5: Excellent leader - study and replicate
3.0-3.5: Solid manager - continuous improvement
2.5-3.0: Needs development - coaching program
<2.5: Critical issue - immediate intervention
```

**Action Threshold:**
- Team avg <2.5: Manager coaching + skip-level meetings
- Individual <2: Potential conflict resolution needed

---

**Question:** "How would you rate your current work-life balance?"

**What We're Measuring:**
- Working hours reasonableness
- After-hours expectations
- Flexibility for personal commitments
- Stress and burnout levels

**Response Scale:**
```
1 = Poor     - Burnt out, unsustainable
2 = Fair     - Manageable but stressed

4 = Excellent - Ideal, sustainable long-term
```

**Why It Matters:**


## Manager's Appraisal

### 🎯 Purpose
Annual performance evaluation system to:
- Recognize achievements
- Identify development needs
- Align individual goals with company objectives
- Inform compensation and promotion decisions
- Support career growth

### 📊 Appraisal Framework

**Frequency:** Annual (December)  
**Process:** Manager-led, HR-supported  
**Timeline:** 4-6 weeks from launch to completion  
**Follow-up:** Quarterly check-ins on development goals

---

### 📝 Appraisal Components

#### 1. Performance Rating (1-4 scale)

**Question:** "Rate the employee's overall job performance"

**Rating Scale:**

```
4 = Exceptional
- Consistently exceeds expectations
- Delivers outstanding results
- Role model for others
- Ready for promotion
- Top 10-15% of workforce

Examples:
- Exceeded annual targets by >30%
- Led critical project to success
- Mentored 3+ junior staff
- Innovated new process saving $50k+
```

```
3 = Strong
- Meets and often exceeds expectations
- Reliable, high-quality work
- Self-directed, minimal supervision
- Backbone of organization
- 70-75% of workforce

Examples:
- Met or beat all quarterly goals
- Completed projects on time
- Positive peer feedback
- Growing in role
```

```
2 = Developing
- Meets some expectations
- Needs improvement in areas
- Requires regular guidance
- Has potential with support
- 10-15% of workforce

Examples:
- Hit 70-80% of targets
- Quality issues occasionally
- Needs clearer direction
- Improving but not there yet
```

```
1 = Needs Improvement
- Does not meet expectations
- Performance concerns
- Requires Performance Improvement Plan
- Risk to team/organization
- Bottom 5% of workforce

Examples:
- Missed most targets (<60%)
- Quality or behavior issues
- Customer complaints
- Team friction
```

**Forced Distribution (Recommended):**
```
Rating 4: 10-15% (Top talent)
Rating 3: 70-80% (Solid performers)
```

### 🚨 Performance Improvement Plans (PIPs)

**When to Use:**
- Consistent underperformance (Rating 1)
- Behavior issues affecting team
- Missed critical deliverables
- After coaching has been attempted

**PIP Structure (90 days):**

```
Week 0: PIP Documentation
- Specific performance gaps
- Expected standards
- Measurable goals
- Consequences if not met

Weeks 1-4: Intensive Support
- Weekly 1-on-1s with manager
- Daily check-ins if needed
- Resources provided
- Clear feedback

Weeks 5-8: Progress Review
- Mid-point assessment
- Adjust if needed
- Celebrate improvements
- Escalate if no progress

Weeks 9-12: Final Evaluation
- Review against goals
- Decision: Exit PIP, extend, or terminate
- Documentation for legal protection

Outcomes:
- 40% successfully exit PIP
- 30% show some improvement (extend)
- 30% fail to improve (termination)
```

**PIP Best Practices:**
- Be clear and direct
- Provide specific, measurable goals
- Offer genuine support (don't set up to fail)
- Document everything
- Involve HR from start
- Prepare for all outcomes

## Data Insights & Interpretations

### 🔍 Correlation Analysis

#### Income vs Attrition

**Finding:** Strong negative correlation (r = -0.72)
- Lower income = Higher attrition risk
- Each $1k increase in monthly salary = ~3% reduction in attrition

**Insights:**
```
Income Bracket    Attrition Rate    Risk Level
<$3k              28%               🔴 Critical
$3k-$5k           18%               🟡 Moderate
$5k-$7k           12%               🟢 Low
$7k+              7%                🟢 Very Low
```

**Recommendations:**
1. **Immediate:** Bring bottom 10% to market rate
2. **Short-term:** Annual COL adjustments
3. **Long-term:** Transparent pay bands, regular benchmarking

---

#### Satisfaction vs Performance

**Finding:** Moderate positive correlation (r = 0.58)
- Satisfied employees perform 23% better on average
- Top performers (Rating 4) have avg satisfaction of 3.2
- Low performers (Rating 1-2) have avg satisfaction of 2.1

**Insights:**
```
The Engagement-Performance Matrix:

High Performance, High Satisfaction (20%):
→ "Stars" - Retain at all costs, promote

High Performance, Low Satisfaction (10%):
→ "Burning Out" - Address quickly, flight risk

Low Performance, High Satisfaction (15%):
→ "Coasters" - Coach for accountability

Low Performance, Low Satisfaction (5%):
→ "Mis-hires" - PIP or exit conversation
```

**Recommendations:**
- Focus on "Burning Out" segment (immediate retention risk)
- Coach "Coasters" for performance improvement
- Study "Stars" - what's working for them?

---

#### Manager Quality vs Team Attrition

**Finding:** Very strong correlation (r = -0.81)
- Best managers (team satisfaction >3.5): 8% attrition
- Average managers (2.5-3.5): 15% attrition
- Poor managers (<2.5): 35% attrition

**Cost Impact:**
```
Poor Manager (10-person team):

- Replacement cost: $50k/employee
- Total impact: $175k/year

Good Manager (10-person team):
- 8% attrition = 0.8 employees leaving
- Replacement cost: $40k
- Savings: $135k/year per good manager
```

**ROI of Manager Training:**
```
Investment:
- Manager training program: $5k/manager
- Ongoing coaching: $3k/manager/year
- Total: $8k

Return (conservative):
- Reduce attrition by 10 percentage points
- Save ~$100k/manager/year
- ROI: 1,150%
```

**Recommendations:**
1. Invest heavily in manager development
2. Make manager quality visible (team satisfaction scores)
3. Promote based on people leadership, not just technical skills
4. Remove managers who consistently underperform
